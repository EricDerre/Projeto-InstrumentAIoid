## 1. How open singing synthesizers are built (pipeline, reusable components and licences, file formats, converters)

### Takeaway
The de-facto open stack in 2026 is **DiffSinger (openvpi, Apache-2.0) models exported to ONNX and run inside OpenUtau (MIT)**: lyrics → dictionary-based phonemizer → *variance model* (phoneme durations, pitch curve, energy/breathiness/voicing/tension) → *acoustic model* (mel-spectrogram, 44.1 kHz, 128 bins, rectified-flow diffusion) → *NSF-HiFiGAN vocoder* → audio, rendered phrase by phrase. NNSVS/ENUNU (MIT, Sinsy-style full-context labels), VOICEVOX (engine LGPL-3.0 dual-licensed, core MIT, with an HTTP singing API) and newer editors (TuneLab MIT with an AI agent/MCP server; DiffScope still without its engine) are the alternatives. Project interchange is solved by LibreSVIP (MIT, a pip-installable Python library) and UtaFormatix3 (Apache-2.0).

### Cited Findings

#### Reference pipeline: DiffSinger (openvpi fork)
- openvpi's DiffSinger refactors the original paper's code and adds: 44.1 kHz output "instead of the original 24 kHz", improved acoustic models and faster diffusion sampling, "variance models and parameters for prediction and control of pitch, energy, breathiness, etc.", and "production compatibility"; production deployment is via OpenUTAU and DiffScope (under development); licence Apache 2.0 — [openvpi/DiffSinger README/LICENSE copy](https://github.com/openvpi/DiffSinger)
- Its listed building blocks: DDPM/DDIM/PNDM/DPM-Solver++/UniPC samplers and Rectified Flow; RoPE transformer encoder; Mix-LN for cross-speaker/lingual learning; HiFi-GAN + NSF and pc-ddsp for waveform reconstruction; RMVPE for pitch extraction; Vocal Remover for harmonic-noise separation — [openvpi/DiffSinger](https://github.com/openvpi/DiffSinger)
- **Variance model** = "automatic parameter generator": input is "phoneme sequence, word division, word durations and music scores"; outputs "phoneme durations, pitch curve and other control parameters". Three independently switchable modules: Duration Predictor, Pitch Predictor, Multi-Variance Predictor — [DiffSinger Best Practices](https://github.com/openvpi/DiffSinger/blob/main/docs/BestPractices.md)
- Supported variance parameters: energy, breathiness, voicing, tension — [DiffSinger Best Practices](https://github.com/openvpi/DiffSinger/blob/main/docs/BestPractices.md)
- **Acoustic model**: three fixed inputs (phoneme sequence, phoneme durations, F0) plus optional speaker IDs (multi-speaker mixing), variance parameters and "transition parameters" from data augmentation (key shift, speed); "The only output of an acoustic model is the mel-spectrogram, which can be converted to waveform … through the vocoder" — [DiffSinger Best Practices](https://github.com/openvpi/DiffSinger/blob/main/docs/BestPractices.md)
- Current defaults in the configuration schema: `audio_sample_rate` 44100, `hop_size` 512, `audio_num_mel_bins` 128 (all "reserved"), `diffusion_type` reflow, `sampling_algorithm` euler, `sampling_steps` 20, shallow-diffusion `T_start` 0.4, `max_updates` 100000, `max_batch_frames` 50000, `max_batch_size` 64, `pl_trainer_precision` 16-mixed, `val_check_interval` 4000 — [DiffSinger Configuration Schemas](https://github.com/openvpi/DiffSinger/blob/main/docs/ConfigurationSchemas.md)
- **G2P / dictionaries**: one dictionary per language, a `.txt` file in which each line maps a syllable to phonemes (tab between syllable and phonemes, spaces between phonemes); ISO 639 codes recommended as language tags; reserved global phonemes `SP` (space) and `AP` (aspiration); language-specific phonemes are prefixed (`zh/a`, `ja/o`, `en/eh`); `merged_phoneme_groups` and `use_lang_id` support multilingual models — [DiffSinger Best Practices](https://github.com/openvpi/DiffSinger/blob/main/docs/BestPractices.md)
- **DS files** are JSON with phoneme sequence, durations, music scores or curves, used for inference/tests; "The current recommended way of using a model for production purposes is to use OpenUTAU for DiffSinger. It can export DS files as well." — [DiffSinger Best Practices](https://github.com/openvpi/DiffSinger/blob/main/docs/BestPractices.md)
- Workflow commands: `scripts/binarize.py` (preprocessing), `scripts/train.py`, `scripts/infer.py variance|acoustic my_song.ds`, and "DiffSinger uses ONNX as the deployment format" via `scripts/export.py variance|acoustic|nsf-hifigan`; "DiffSinger requires Python 3.10 or later"; PyTorch ">= 2.4.0" recommended — [DiffSinger Getting Started](https://github.com/openvpi/DiffSinger/blob/main/docs/GettingStarted.md)
- Pinned training dependencies: `librosa<0.10.0`, `lightning~=2.3.0`, `numpy<2.0.0`, `onnx>=1.21.0`, `onnxsim>=0.6.5`, `praat-parselmouth==0.4.3`, `pyworld==0.3.4`, plus resampy, scipy>=1.10.0, tensorboard, torchmetrics; PyTorch installed manually — [DiffSinger requirements.txt](https://github.com/openvpi/DiffSinger/blob/main/requirements.txt)
- **Vocoder**: the DiffSinger Community Vocoders Project provides "a universal pre-trained NSF-HiFiGAN vocoder" (~50 MB); fine-tuning it on the target dataset is "highly recommended"; an ultra-light pc-ddsp vocoder is an "unrecommended option" — [DiffSinger Best Practices](https://github.com/openvpi/DiffSinger/blob/main/docs/BestPractices.md)
- **Pitch / feature extraction**: parselmouth is the default (DSP, "runs fast on CPU"); RMVPE is "recommended" (NN-based, CUDA-accelerated, "state-of-the-art … for singing voice"); Harvest (WORLD) is also supported; harmonic-noise separation via WORLD or Vocal Remover (recommended) — [DiffSinger Best Practices](https://github.com/openvpi/DiffSinger/blob/main/docs/BestPractices.md)
- The original DiffSinger (MoonInTheRiver) is MIT-licensed — [MoonInTheRiver/DiffSinger LICENSE copy](https://github.com/MoonInTheRiver/DiffSinger)

#### OpenUtau (the editor/host platform)
- "a free, open-source editor made for the UTAU community"; MIT; MIDI editor with VSQX (Vocaloid 4) import; phonemizers for VCV, CVVC, Arpasing etc. in "English, Japanese, Chinese, Korean, Russian and more"; built-in WORLDLINE-R resampler with curve tuning; pre-rendering before playback; ENUNU support; plugin system; "it will not replace your digital audio workstation" — [OpenUtau README/LICENSE copy](https://github.com/stakira/OpenUtau)
- Renderer IDs in current source: CLASSIC, WORLDLINE-R, WORLDLINE-R2, WORLDLINE-R1.1, ENUNU, VOGEN, DIFFSINGER, VOICEVOX — [OpenUtau Renderers.cs](https://github.com/stakira/OpenUtau/blob/master/OpenUtau.Core/Render/Renderers.cs)
- The renderer contract is **phrase-based**: `Task<RenderResult> Render(RenderPhrase phrase, …)` returns `float[] samples` with `leadingMs`, `positionMs`, `estimatedLengthMs`; optional `LoadRenderedPitch` (pitch as MIDI semitones per tick, with voiced masks), "real curves", `SupportsExpression`, `GetSuggestedExpressions`, `PhrasePadding` — [OpenUtau IRenderer.cs](https://github.com/stakira/OpenUtau/blob/master/OpenUtau.Core/Render/IRenderer.cs); dynamics are applied assuming 44,100 samples per second (`/ 1000 * 44100`) — [OpenUtau Renderers.cs](https://github.com/stakira/OpenUtau/blob/master/OpenUtau.Core/Render/Renderers.cs)
- Adding a new engine (wiki, using DiffSinger as the example): design a singer package (recommended "classic" format: `character.txt` + `character.yaml` with `singer_type` and `default_phonemizer`; DiffSinger files prefixed `ds`: `dsdur`, `dspitch`, `dsvariance`, `dsconfig`, `dsvocoder`), subclass `USinger`, implement `IRenderer`, optionally write phonemizers (ML phonemizers inherit `MachineLearningPhonemizer`) — [OpenUtau wiki: Adding support for a new synthesis engine](https://github.com/stakira/OpenUtau/wiki/Adding-support-for-a-new-synthesis-engine)
- Phonemizer plugin API (C#, "Experimental. Subject to change."): `Phoneme[] Process(Note[] notes, Note? prevNeighbour, Note? nextNeighbour)`; must support phonetic hints like `read[r iy d]` and extender notes `+n` — [OpenUtau API README](https://github.com/stakira/OpenUtau/blob/master/OpenUtau.Core/Api/README.md)
- Latest release 0.1.573 Beta dated 08 Oct (2026 by context; preceded by 0.1.572.3 Alpha 06 Oct and 0.1.572.4 Alpha 07 Oct): "A model that DirectML rejects now falls back to CPU instead of failing the render"; missing engine packages (WORLDLINE-R2, hifisampler) installable in-app; fix for loading Voicevox rendered pitch — [OpenUtau releases](https://github.com/stakira/OpenUtau/releases)
- The DiffSinger renderer was developed in xunmengshe/OpenUtau and "has been merged to the official repo"; that fork's Windows "diffsingerpack" ships the nsf_hifigan vocoder — [xunmengshe/OpenUtau README copy](https://github.com/xunmengshe/OpenUtau)
- A Brazilian Portuguese phonemizer exists: "PT-BR CVC" for CVC voicebanks, built-in G2P dictionary, "made with BRAPA" — [OpenUtau wiki: Phonemizers](https://github.com/stakira/OpenUtau/wiki/Phonemizers)

#### NNSVS, ENUNU, Sinsy
- NNSVS: "Neural network-based singing voice synthesis library for research" (paper arXiv 2210.15987); uSFGAN inference adapted from HN-UnifiedSourceFilterGAN; diffusion code adapted from DiffSinger; MIT — [nnsvs README/LICENSE copy](https://github.com/nnsvs/nnsvs)
- ENUNU: UTAU plugin that makes NNSVS singing models usable like UTAU voicebanks; dev environment Windows 10, Python 3.12, CUDA 13.0; its full-context label spec "differs from Sinsy's" (no phrase, dynamics, bar or beat info); MIT (2020–2025 oatsu) — [oatsu-gh/ENUNU README/LICENSE copy](https://github.com/oatsu-gh/ENUNU)
- Sinsy: "released under the Modified BSD license"; latest version 0.92 (25 Dec 2015) — [Sinsy readme](https://sinsy.sourceforge.net/readme_sinsy.php)

#### VOICEVOX (does it sing? yes)
- Engine singing API (sample in README): POST `/sing_frame_audio_query?speaker=6000` with a score of notes `{ "key": <MIDI number or null>, "frame_length": n, "lyric": "ド" }` → query JSON; POST `/frame_synthesis?speaker=3001` → WAV; default frame rate 93.75 Hz; the first note must be silent; speakers for the query must be styles of type `sing`/`singing_teacher`, for synthesis of type `frame_decode` — [VOICEVOX ENGINE README copy](https://github.com/VOICEVOX/voicevox_engine)
- Engine "developed with Python 3.11.9"; licence: "LGPL v3 and a separate licence that does not require source disclosure" (dual) — [VOICEVOX ENGINE README copy](https://github.com/VOICEVOX/voicevox_engine)
- VOICEVOX CORE ships a C API dynamic library and a Python wheel; source and build artifacts are MIT, but prebuilt core libraries below version 0.16 are under a different licence — [VOICEVOX CORE README copy](https://github.com/VOICEVOX/voicevox_core)
- A prototype song feature arrived in VOICEVOX 0.16.0 (31 Jan 2024) with five singers; characters have their own terms (companion notes) — [Forest Watch](https://forest.watch.impress.co.jp/docs/news/1567478.html)

### Inferences
- The cleanest reuse boundary for a Python app is the **ONNX export**: training needs PyTorch, but rendering a DiffSinger voicebank only needs ONNX Runtime + NumPy plus re-implementing the orchestration that OpenUtau's C# DiffSinger renderer/phonemizer performs (dictionary G2P → linguistic/duration → pitch → variance → acoustic → vocoder). dsinfer (C++, Apache-2.0) is a second reference implementation of that orchestration.
- A realistic first milestone is to **use OpenUtau as the editor and renderer** (MIT, already supports DiffSinger, ENUNU, VOICEVOX and Portuguese CVC) and make InstrumentAIoid generate/convert projects (USTX/MIDI/MusicXML via LibreSVIP) and handle the LLM, instruments and mixing, instead of writing a piano-roll editor first.
- VOICEVOX's engine is the closest architectural match to the user's existing FastAPI design (a local HTTP synthesis server with a score → query → synthesis singing API) and is worth copying as a pattern, but its LGPL-3.0 licence and per-character voice terms make it less attractive to embed than DiffSinger/OpenUtau code.
- Because DiffSinger/OpenUtau render whole phrases (diffusion with 20 Euler steps by default), any DAW integration must pre-render and cache audio rather than synthesise sample-by-sample in the audio callback (see section 3).
- Copyleft hotspots to avoid in a closed/commercial build: praat-parselmouth (GPLv3; DiffSinger's default pitch extractor, so prefer RMVPE/harvest in a shipped product), espeak-ng (GPL-3.0) for G2P, the GPL ENUNU training kit, and AGPL ddsp-singing-vocoders.

## 2. Creating your own voicebank or instrument bank: tools, data amounts, training time, 4 GB VRAM vs Colab/cloud

### Takeaway
A DiffSinger voicebank is built by recording mono singing, slicing it (AudioSlicer), labelling words/phonemes (MinLabel → MFA/SOFA/HubertFA → vLabeler fixes), extracting notes and pitch (SOME/GAME, RMVPE), binarizing, training an acoustic and a variance model, fine-tuning the community NSF-HiFiGAN vocoder (~2,000 steps) and exporting ONNX for OpenUtau. No official minimum dataset size was found; reference points are the original paper's 117 songs ≈5.89 h (trained on one V100 for ≈28 h total) and openvpi's default 100k training steps. Training on a 4 GB GTX 1650 is at best marginal (official configs target 16–24 GB GPUs); free Colab (≤12 h sessions, GPU type not guaranteed) or rented GPUs are the realistic training path, while ONNX inference runs fine on CPU/DirectML.

### Cited Findings

#### Dataset format and labelling pipeline
- Raw dataset = `wavs/` + `transcriptions.csv`; acoustic training needs columns `name`, `ph_seq`, `ph_dur` (seconds); WAV or FLAC, mono recommended; multi-speaker and multi-language datasets supported — [DiffSinger Best Practices](https://github.com/openvpi/DiffSinger/blob/main/docs/BestPractices.md)
- Variance training additionally needs `ph_num` (duration prediction) and `note_seq` + `note_dur` (pitch prediction); the recommended way is to extend an acoustic dataset, or to build it from DS files (`binarization_args.prefer_ds: true`) exported from OpenUtau — [DiffSinger Best Practices](https://github.com/openvpi/DiffSinger/blob/main/docs/BestPractices.md)
- MakeDiffSinger provides the standard pipelines "acoustic-forced-alignment: make dataset from scratch with MFA" and "variance-temp-solution"; its tools are AudioSlicer, MinLabel (word transcriptions in `.lab`) and SlurCutter (edit MIDI in `.ds`) — [openvpi/MakeDiffSinger README copy](https://github.com/openvpi/MakeDiffSinger)
- openvpi dataset-tools (Qt/C++ apps): MinLabel (G2P for Mandarin/Cantonese/Japanese), SlurCutter (piano roll with F0), AudioSlicer, LyricFA (Chinese only), HubertFA (HuBERT phoneme alignment → TextGrid), GameInfer (GAME audio-to-MIDI, 4-model ONNX); Windows is the "primary" platform "with DirectML GPU acceleration"; FoxBreatheLabeler annotates breaths (AP) on SOFA TextGrids; dependencies include FoxBreatheLabeler (AGPL-3.0) and fftw3 (GPL-2.0) — [openvpi/dataset-tools README copy](https://github.com/openvpi/dataset-tools)
- AudioSlicer 2.0: RMS silence detection; "over 400x faster than real-time on an Intel i7 8750H CPU"; MIT — [openvpi/audio-slicer](https://github.com/openvpi/audio-slicer)
- OpenUtau's voicebank wiki lists vLabeler (manual phoneme labels), SOFA (automated labelling), LabelMakr (GUI for SOFA) and PixPrucer's "SVS Singing voice database" tutorial; it says a GPU is needed for training, recommends DiffSinger over ENUNU (higher quality, more resources available), links DiffSinger4Colab and the DiffTrainer local kit, and says the dataset must contain all phonemes of the target language (paraphrased by the fetch tool) — [OpenUtau wiki: Voicebank development](https://github.com/stakira/OpenUtau/wiki/Voicebank-development)
- vLabeler: "open-source voice labeling application", Compose Multiplatform, builds for Windows/macOS/Ubuntu, Apache-2.0 — [sdercolin/vlabeler README copy](https://github.com/sdercolin/vlabeler)
- Montreal Forced Aligner: Kaldi-based CLI aligner, installed via `conda install -c conda-forge montreal-forced-aligner`; dev environment pins `python=3.11`; MFA 3.x paper at Interspeech 2026; MIT — [MFA README copy](https://github.com/MontrealCorpusTools/Montreal-Forced-Aligner)
- MFA pretrained Portuguese acoustic models: "Portuguese MFA acoustic model v2_0_0" and "v2_0_0a" (MFA phoneset, CC BY 4.0) and "Portuguese CV acoustic model v2_0_0" (Epitran phoneset, CC-0) — [MFA models: acoustic](https://mfa-models.readthedocs.io/en/latest/acoustic/index.html)

#### Notes and pitch extraction
- SOME (Singing-Oriented MIDI Extractor): "9x faster than real-time on an i5 12400 CPU, and 300x on a 3080Ti GPU"; "can achieve good results with only 3 hours of training data" (for training SOME itself); produces non-integer MIDI values "specially suitable for DiffSinger variance labeling"; `batch_infer.py` writes `note_seq`/`note_dur` into transcriptions.csv; optional RMVPE; MIT — [openvpi/SOME README copy](https://github.com/openvpi/SOME)
- GAME (Generative Adaptive MIDI Extractor) is "the upgraded successor of SOME": D3PM generative boundary extraction trading quality vs speed, aligns notes to known word boundaries, "works on dirty or separated voice mixed with noise, reverb or even accompaniments", multilingual; "tested under Python 3.12, PyTorch 2.8.0, CUDA 12.9, Lightning 2.6.1" — [openvpi/GAME README copy](https://github.com/openvpi/GAME)
- RMVPE: "A Robust Model for Vocal Pitch Estimation in Polyphonic Music" (arXiv 2306.15412), Apache-2.0 — [Dream-High/RMVPE](https://github.com/Dream-High/RMVPE)

#### Training cost and hardware
- Original DiffSinger paper: PopCS = 117 Chinese pop songs, ≈5.89 h, one female singer, 24 kHz/16-bit; trained on 1 NVIDIA V100 with batch size 48; warm-up stage (auxiliary decoder 160k steps + boundary predictor 30k steps) ≈16 h; main stage 160k steps ≈12 h — [DiffSinger paper, arXiv 2105.02446](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2105.02446)
- openvpi defaults: `max_updates` 100,000 steps; AMP "16-mixed" by default, which "can accelerate training and save GPU memory"; `accumulate_grad_batches` "can simulate a larger batch size with a lower GPU memory cost"; full shallow-diffusion training "can consume more memory", so "in resource-limited situations, the two decoders can be trained separately"; fine-tuning from a checkpoint (`finetune_ckpt_path`) and parameter freezing save time/memory — [DiffSinger Best Practices](https://github.com/openvpi/DiffSinger/blob/main/docs/BestPractices.md), [Configuration Schemas](https://github.com/openvpi/DiffSinger/blob/main/docs/ConfigurationSchemas.md)
- Vocoder fine-tuning (SingingVocoders): about 2,000 real steps (the display shows 4,000) are enough on small data; freezing the MPD module may help; do not use bf16; use 44.1 kHz audio; the published recommended configs are for 24 GB and 16 GB GPUs; exported weights work in DiffSinger, DDSP-SVC, Diffusion-SVC, so-vits-svc, and can be converted to ONNX for OpenUtau — [openvpi/SingingVocoders README copy](https://github.com/openvpi/SingingVocoders)
- Community Colab notebooks: DiffSinger training (accepts lab+wav NNSVS format, csv+wav DiffSinger format, or `.ds`), a PC-NSF/NSF-HiFiGAN vocoder fine-tuning notebook, and a work-in-progress SOFA training notebook ("please either don't use it at all or use it with caution"); the local GUI moved to DiffTrainer — [MLo7Ghinsan/DiffSinger_colab_notebook_MLo7 README copy](https://github.com/MLo7Ghinsan/DiffSinger_colab_notebook_MLo7)
- Free Colab: "notebooks can run for at most 12 hours", idle runtimes time out, "The types of GPUs and TPUs that are available in Colab vary over time", limits are dynamic and unpublished; Pro/Pro+/Pay As You Go give more compute, Pro+ up to 24 h continuous execution — [Colab FAQ](https://research.google.com/colaboratory/faq.html)
- ENUNU Training Kit (Windows 10/11, batch-file workflow) builds NNSVS/ENUNU models from a singing database with UST + LAB + WAV (16-bit, 44.1 or 48 kHz); the README warns (in Japanese) that model training takes time and vocoder training takes a very long time; v2.0.0 (2025-12-26) moved to Python 3.12.10 and added HN-uSFGAN; v2.1.0 (2026-07-25) made acoustic-model training 10–20x faster — [oatsu-gh/enunu_training_kit README copy](https://github.com/oatsu-gh/enunu_training_kit)
- Reference dataset sizes: GTSinger has 80.59 h of studio singing plus 16.16 h of paired speech (NeurIPS 2024 Spotlight) — [AaronZ345/GTSinger README copy](https://github.com/AaronZ345/GTSinger); SoulX-Singer (zero-shot SVS conditioned on F0 or MIDI score) was trained on "42,000+ hours" of Mandarin/English/Cantonese — [Soul-AILab/SoulX-Singer README copy](https://github.com/Soul-AILab/SoulX-Singer)

#### Instrument banks (sample-based and neural)
- FluidSynth: real-time SoundFont 2 synthesizer, LGPL-2.1 — [FluidSynth README/LICENSE copy](https://github.com/FluidSynth/fluidsynth); sfizz: SFZ parser/synth C++ library (plugins via sfizz-ui), BSD-2-Clause — [sfztools/sfizz README/LICENSE copy](https://github.com/sfztools/sfizz)
- DDSP-VST: VST3/AU plugins and desktop apps built with JUCE and DDSP, verified in Ableton/Logic/FL Studio/Reaper, with a "Train a new model" link (Colab); Apache-2.0 — [magenta/ddsp-vst README/LICENSE copy](https://github.com/magenta/ddsp-vst); MIDI-DDSP (hierarchical MIDI → expressive instrument audio) and DDSP-Piano are Apache-2.0 — [magenta/midi-ddsp](https://github.com/magenta/midi-ddsp), [lrenault/ddsp-piano](https://github.com/lrenault/ddsp-piano)
- RAVE (Realtime Audio Variational autoEncoder): trainable on custom data, Colab available; RAVE, nn~ and RAVE VST are CC BY-NC 4.0 (non-commercial); RAVE VST: "You should not expect any update or support" — [acids-ircam/RAVE](https://github.com/acids-ircam/RAVE), [acids-ircam/rave_vst](https://github.com/acids-ircam/rave_vst)
- Neutone SDK (LGPL-2.1) wraps PyTorch audio models for the free Neutone FX (real-time) and Neutone Gen (non-real-time, beta) VST3/AU host plugins, handling "variable buffer sizes, sample rate conversion, delay compensation, and control parameter handling" — [Neutone/neutone_sdk README copy](https://github.com/Neutone/neutone_sdk)
- ACE-Step 1.5 (MIT, 2026; XL 4B DiT released 2026-04-02): text/lyrics-to-song model with "1000+ instruments and styles"; LoRA training "8 songs, 1 hour on 3090 (12GB VRAM)"; for ≤6 GB VRAM it recommends the "2B turbo" DiT with the LM disabled, INT8 quantization and full CPU offload; requires Python 3.11–3.12 — [ace-step/ACE-Step-1.5 README/LICENSE copy](https://github.com/ace-step/ACE-Step-1.5)
- Magenta RealTime 2: open-weights real-time music model (mrt2_small 230M, mrt2_base 2.4B) with an AUv3 plugin example; "Real-time streaming requires Apple Silicon" — [magenta/magenta-realtime README copy](https://github.com/magenta/magenta-realtime)

### Inferences
- On the user's GTX 1650 (4 GB), full DiffSinger training at default settings (max_batch_frames 50,000) will likely run out of memory; it may be possible with AMP, much smaller `max_batch_frames`/`max_batch_size`, gradient accumulation, separate decoder training and fine-tuning from a pretrained checkpoint, but the practical plan is: label and preprocess locally (CPU tools are fast: AudioSlicer 400x, SOME 9x real-time on CPU), train acoustic/variance models and fine-tune the vocoder on Colab or a rented 16–24 GB GPU, then run ONNX inference locally.
- Given the original paper's ≈5.9 h single-singer corpus and SOME's "3 hours" figure for its own training, plan for at least an hour of clean, phonetically complete singing for a usable personal voicebank and several hours for a good one — this is an inference, not a documented threshold.
- For Brazilian Portuguese, the alignment step is the bottleneck: MFA has CC BY 4.0 Portuguese acoustic models, but a DiffSinger Portuguese dictionary/phoneme set must be designed (DiffSinger dictionaries are simple syllable→phoneme text files, and OpenUtau's PT-BR CVC G2P shows the approach).
- For "instrument banks", the fastest route is sample-based (record multisamples → SFZ for sfizz, or SF2 for FluidSynth), which is real-time and permissively licensed (sfizz BSD-2); neural instruments (DDSP-style) are a second step; RAVE-family tools are non-commercial.

### Gaps
- No official or community-documented minimum amount of singing data for a DiffSinger voicebank was found (OpenUtau wiki gives none; the PixPrucer guide was not read).
- No measured DiffSinger training times or VRAM use on a 4 GB card (or any Turing card) were found.
- SOFA's accuracy, licence and current status were not verified; HubertFA/LyricFA language coverage beyond Chinese was not checked.
- DDSP-VST training time/data requirements and the licence of its pretrained weights were not read.

## 3. DAW integration (Cubase, FL Studio, REAPER): plug-in frameworks, ARA2, bridge plug-ins, in-plug-in inference, simplest path, licence implications

### Takeaway
The simplest path is offline: render WAV (and stems) from the standalone app and drag them into the DAW, plus MIDI/MusicXML import-export. The commercial vocal synths (ACE Studio's ACE Bridge, VOCALOID6, Synthesizer V Studio 2) instead ship a VST3/AU/AAX "bridge" plug-in using ARA2 for tempo/playback sync — but no evidence of ARA support in FL Studio was found (users were still requesting it in April 2026), while Cubase (since 10), REAPER (since 5) and Studio One support ARA. Licensing became much easier: the VST3 SDK has been MIT since VST 3.8 (October 2025), the ARA SDK is Apache-2.0, CLAP and clap-wrapper are MIT; JUCE is AGPLv3 or a paid/free-tier commercial licence (JUCE 9: Starter free up to US$20k revenue). Neural vocals cannot run sample-by-sample in the audio callback; the plug-in should play pre-rendered, cached audio from a separate engine process (as Neutone Gen/ACE Bridge-style designs do), reserving real-time inference (anira/RTNeural/ONNX Runtime) for small models.

#### Simplest path (companion-notes evidence)
- Even VOCALOID's free Editor Lite is standalone-only, so its workflow is "write vocals standalone, export WAV, import into the DAW" (companion notes) — [vocaloid.com specs](https://www.vocaloid.com/en/vocaloid6/specs/)

### Inferences
- **Stage 1 (weeks)**: standalone render to WAV per track/stem + MIDI/MusicXML/USTX export; the user drags files into Cubase, FL Studio or REAPER. Works identically in all three DAWs and needs no plug-in SDK.
- **Stage 2 (bridge)**: a thin VST3 (and/or CLAP) instrument plug-in that only (a) reads host tempo/transport, (b) sends the song position to the local Python engine over IPC (localhost HTTP/WebSocket or shared memory), and (c) streams back pre-rendered, cached audio. This mirrors ACE Bridge/Neutone Gen and avoids running PyTorch in the DAW. With VST3 now MIT, iPlug2 (zlib) or DPF (ISC) let a solo dev ship a closed or open plug-in for free; JUCE is free only under AGPLv3 or the Starter tier (≤US$20k revenue). nih-plug's VST3 path implies GPLv3 and the project is in maintenance mode.
- **Stage 3 (ARA2)**: worth it only for Cubase/REAPER/Studio One users; it adds substantial C++ complexity and FL Studio would still need the transport-sync fallback. Yamaha's 2026 Cubase 15 ARA2 notice shows ARA integrations can break with host updates.
- Real-time budget: at 48 kHz a 256-sample buffer allows ≈5.3 ms per block (the figure in anira's example), so only small models (DDSP-style instruments, amp-sim-sized networks) belong in the audio thread; DiffSinger-class vocals must be rendered ahead of playback.
- Cubase does not appear on the CLAP host list, so a CLAP-first plug-in must also ship VST3 (natively or via clap-wrapper).

### Gaps
- No primary source was read for ARA support in Cubase 15/REAPER 7 specifically (only Celemony's historical list), nor whether REAPER/Cubase support ARA for instrument (not effect) plug-ins as used by vocal synths.
- How ACE Bridge, VOCALOID6 and Synthesizer V move audio between editor and plug-in (in-process vs IPC) was not documented in fetched sources; Dreamtonics' plug-in manual page could not be read.
- The licence of the Rust VST3 bindings used by nih-plug's Codeberg fork after the VST3 SDK went MIT was not checked.
- Whether FL Studio's current version supports CLAP instruments with full transport info was not re-verified beyond the Nov 2024 host list.

## 4. Python prototyping toolkit and Python 3.14 compatibility on Windows (October 2026)

### Takeaway
Python 3.14 itself is no longer the blocker for PyTorch or ONNX Runtime: torch 2.14.1 has Windows cp314 wheels (including a CUDA 12.6 build) and onnxruntime 1.30.0 ships cp314 wheels; pedalboard, DawDreamer, numba, parselmouth and all pure-Python libraries (mido, pretty_midi, music21, LibreSVIP, FastAPI, uvicorn, ollama) work too. The blockers are the singing-synthesis tools: DiffSinger pins numpy<2.0 (wheels only to cp312), pyworld==0.3.4 (Windows wheels only to cp311) and praat-parselmouth==0.4.3 (to cp312); basic-pitch pulls TensorFlow<2.15.1 on Windows, and TensorFlow has no cp314 wheels; ACE-Step needs 3.11–3.12. Recommendation: keep a **Python 3.11 (or 3.12) environment for training/labelling tools** and run the app layer on 3.12 (or 3.14 if desired), with separate virtual environments.

### Inferences
- DiffSinger training/tools on Windows: use **Python 3.11** (every pin has prebuilt wheels: numpy 1.26.4, pyworld 0.3.4, parselmouth 0.4.3, onnxsim); Python 3.12 works only if pyworld 0.3.4 is compiled with MSVC; 3.13/3.14 are impractical without patching the pins (numpy<2.0 has no wheels there).
- Runtime app (FastAPI + Ollama client + ONNX Runtime DirectML/CPU + mido/pretty_midi + pedalboard/DawDreamer + LibreSVIP): runs on 3.12 and probably on 3.14 given the wheels found; but standardising the whole project on 3.11 or 3.12 avoids two interpreters in the PyInstaller build. Use separate venvs (e.g., with uv) for "app", "training/labelling" and "Colab".
- For GPU inference in Python on the GTX 1650: torch cu126 builds (2.14.x) are the safe choice; from 2.15 only CUDA 13 builds remain, which still cover Turing (sm_75). For ONNX inference on Windows without a CUDA setup, DirectML is the route OpenUtau and openvpi dataset-tools already use (OpenUtau now falls back to CPU when DirectML rejects a model), but the onnxruntime-directml PyPI releases lag the core package (1.24.4 vs 1.30.0).
- basic-pitch on Python ≥3.12 on Windows needs a workaround (install without its TensorFlow dependency and use the ONNX model, or use NeuralNote as a plug-in); SOME/GAME are the singing-specific alternatives anyway.
- DawDreamer and pedalboard are GPL-3.0: fine for a personal or GPL app, but distributing a closed-source app that bundles them would trigger GPL obligations.

## 5. Role of a local LLM via Ollama (lyrics, syllabification, text-to-MIDI, expression control) and models that fit 4 GB VRAM / 16 GB RAM

### Takeaway
A local LLM is useful as a **composer's assistant and command layer**, not as the synthesizer: writing/adapting lyrics, proposing melodies as structured JSON notes (converted to MIDI/USTX by code), and mapping natural-language requests ("softer, breathier in the chorus") to concrete parameters (DiffSinger energy/breathiness/tension curves, OpenUtau expressions) through Ollama's JSON-schema structured outputs. Research shows small LLMs can be fine-tuned for text-to-MIDI (MIDI-LLM on Llama 3.2 1B beats Text2midi), and TuneLab already exposes an AI agent and an MCP server for SVS-editor control. Mistral 7B (v0.3, May 2024, 4.4 GB) is dated and does not fit in 4 GB of VRAM; 2–4 B models such as ministral-3:3b (Apache-2.0), qwen3:4b, qwen3.5:4b/2b, llama3.2:3b or gemma3:4b fit or nearly fit the GTX 1650, with 8–9 B models possible via CPU+GPU split in 16 GB RAM.

### Cited Findings

#### Models available in Ollama (library pages read 2026-10-08)
- `mistral:7b` = v0.3 (dated 05/22/2024), download 4.4 GB, 32K context, Apache licence, "Updated 1 year ago" — [ollama.com/library/mistral](https://ollama.com/library/mistral)
- `ministral-3`: 3b 3.0 GB, 8b 6.0 GB, 14b 9.1 GB; 256K context; vision + tools ("native function calling with JSON output", multilingual); Apache 2.0; "Updated 9 months ago" — [ollama.com/library/ministral-3](https://ollama.com/library/ministral-3)
- `qwen3`: 0.6b 523 MB, 1.7b 1.4 GB, 4b 2.5 GB (256K context), 8b 5.2 GB, 14b 9.3 GB — [ollama.com/library/qwen3](https://ollama.com/library/qwen3)
- `qwen3.5` (vision, tools, thinking; 256K context): 0.8b 1.2–1.3 GB, 2b 2.7–3.1 GB, 4b 3.3–4.0 GB, 9b 6.6–7.6 GB; tags "updated 2 days ago" — [ollama.com/library/qwen3.5](https://ollama.com/library/qwen3.5)
- `gemma3`: 1b 815 MB (32K), 4b 3.3 GB (128K, text+image), 12b 8.1 GB; Gemma Terms of Use — [ollama.com/library/gemma3](https://ollama.com/library/gemma3)
- `llama3.2`: 1b 1.3 GB, 3b 2.0 GB, 128K context, "2 years ago" — [ollama.com/library/llama3.2](https://ollama.com/library/llama3.2)
- The Ollama catalogue shows 245 models; its featured/new entries in Oct 2026 are mostly ≥27B or cloud-only (e.g., qwen3.6 27b/35b, qwen3.8 27b, nemotron3 33b, deepseek-v4.1-flash/glm-5.3/mistral-large-4 as cloud models), with few small chat models (tev1 0.8b/4b, described for decision/classification) — [ollama.com/search](https://ollama.com/search)

#### Structured outputs (for JSON notes/parameters)
- "Structured outputs let you enforce a JSON schema on model responses": pass `format: "json"` or a JSON schema (e.g., from Pydantic `model_json_schema()`) to `/api/chat`, validate with `model_validate_json`; "Lower the temperature (e.g., set it to 0)"; "It is ideal to also pass the JSON schema as a string in the prompt"; also available via the OpenAI-compatible API (`response_format`); not supported on Ollama Cloud — [Ollama docs: structured outputs](https://docs.ollama.com/capabilities/structured-outputs)

#### Text-to-MIDI and LLM-in-the-loop examples (2024–2026)
- MIDI-LLM (Wu, Carlton, Miyakawa, Kim, Donahue, Huang; arXiv 2511.03942, 6 Nov 2025): adapts Llama 3.2 **1B** by adding MIDI tokens to the text vocabulary; two-stage training (continued pretraining on music text + MIDIs, then text–MIDI SFT); "outperforms the recent Text2midi model in both text control and musical quality"; integrates with vLLM; user study with 58 Hookpad Aria users — [arXiv 2511.03942](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.03942v1); listed at NeurIPS 2025 — [NeurIPS virtual](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2025/123799)
- Text2midi (Bhandari, Roy, Wang, Puri, Colton, Herremans; arXiv 2412.16526, Dec 2024): "a pretrained LLM encoder to process captions" conditioning "an autoregressive transformer decoder to produce MIDI sequences"; code and samples released at AMAAI-Lab/Text2midi — [arXiv 2412.16526](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.16526v1); AAAI 2025 — [ML Anthology](https://mlanthology.org/aaai/2025/bhandari2025aaai-text)
- TuneLab (SVS editor) ships "built-in scripting and an AI agent"; `tunelab mcp` exposes the agent's commands (read the project, run scripts, change settings, trigger editor actions) to "any MCP-capable AI client" — [TuneLab README copy](https://github.com/LiuYunPlayer/TuneLab)
- ACE Studio 2.0's AI features: 140 voice models with six automatable expression parameters (Formant, Energy, Tension, Falsetto, Air, Breath), analysis of the user's own vocal into lyrics/MIDI, Choir Mode, orchestral instrument models, generation from MIDI, generative kits, stem splitter, voice cloning; lyric generation/LLM agent not mentioned — [Mixdown review](https://mixdownmag.com.au/reviews/review-ace-studio-2-0/)
- ACE-Step 1.5 (MIT) pairs a DiT audio decoder with an optional language model (`acestep-5Hz-lm-1.7B`); with ≤6 GB VRAM the LM is disabled by default — [ACE-Step-1.5 README copy](https://github.com/ace-step/ACE-Step-1.5)

### Inferences
- Replace `ollama run mistral` + `/api/generate` with `/api/chat` + JSON-schema `format` (Pydantic models such as `Song{tempo, key, sections[], notes[{pitch, start_beat, dur_beats, syllable}]}`), then turn the JSON into MIDI with mido/pretty_midi and into USTX/other formats with LibreSVIP. Generation should be validated in code (range, scale, syllable count), because general-purpose LLMs are not trained melody models like MIDI-LLM.
- Syllabification for singing (especially Portuguese) should be done by deterministic rules/dictionaries (as DiffSinger dictionaries and OpenUtau's PT-BR CVC G2P do), with the LLM only proposing lyric text and stress/syllable counts per note; LLM syllable splitting is unreliable for alignment-critical data.
- Natural-language expression control maps well onto DiffSinger's explicit controls (energy, breathiness, voicing, tension curves; speaker mix; key shift) and onto OpenUtau/TuneLab expressions; an LLM emitting small parameter-edit JSON (or MCP tool calls, as TuneLab supports) is a practical design.
- Model choice for the GTX 1650: `ministral-3:3b` (3.0 GB, Apache-2.0, function calling) is the natural in-family replacement for Mistral 7B; `qwen3:4b` (2.5 GB) and `llama3.2:3b` (2.0 GB) leave VRAM headroom; `qwen3.5:4b` (3.3–4.0 GB) is borderline; 8–9 B models (qwen3:8b 5.2 GB, ministral-3:8b 6.0 GB) will split between GPU and the 16 GB of system RAM and run slower. VRAM must also be shared with ONNX/PyTorch voice inference, so LLM and synthesis should not run at the same time on the GPU (or the LLM should run on CPU).
- MIDI-LLM shows a 1B model can be specialised for text-to-MIDI; a later project phase could fine-tune a small model (on Colab) for melody/lyric alignment, but this is research-grade work.

### Gaps
- Licences of qwen3/qwen3.5 and llama3.2 were not stated on the Ollama pages read; Gemma's terms were not read in full.
- No benchmark of Portuguese lyric quality or singable syllable control for these small models was found.
- Weights/licence availability for MIDI-LLM and Text2midi (beyond "code released" for Text2midi) were not confirmed; ChatMusician/SongComposer-style lyric-to-melody models were not researched.
- No measured tokens/second for these models on a GTX 1650 + Ryzen 7 5800H was found.

## 6. Legal and ethical constraints (voice rights and consent, character licences, model/dataset licences, AI-content rules)

### Takeaway
Only voices recorded with explicit, documented consent can be used to build voicebanks; Brazilian law protects the reproduction of the human voice (Constitution art. 5 XXVIII-a) and treats biometric data as sensitive (LGPD), and the open-source tools themselves forbid cloning voices without consent. Commercial character voices cannot be used as training data: Crypton's EULA (Super Pack) explicitly prohibits using its products or synthesized vocals "as training data for the development of generative AI", while songs made with the software may be used commercially (with some exceptions). Character images are CC BY-NC/PCL (commercial use needs permission); Kasane Teto's UTAU voice is free for non-commercial use, with commercial use of the library needing permission and redistribution prohibited, and AI training not addressed. Several common assets are non-commercial (community NSF-HiFiGAN vocoder reported as CC BY-NC-SA 4.0, M4Singer and GTSinger datasets CC BY-NC-SA 4.0, RAVE CC BY-NC 4.0). From 2 August 2026 the EU AI Act requires providers to mark synthetic audio as AI-generated in a machine-readable way.

### Cited Findings

#### Voice rights and consent
- Brazilian Constitution art. 5, X: "são invioláveis a intimidade, a vida privada, a honra e a imagem das pessoas, assegurado o direito a indenização…"; art. 5, XXVIII, a: assures "a proteção … à reprodução da imagem e voz humanas, inclusive nas atividades desportivas" — [Constituição Federal (Planalto)](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/constituicao/constituicao.htm)
- LGPD art. 5, II defines sensitive personal data to include "dado genético ou biométrico, quando vinculado a uma pessoa natural" — [Lei 13.709/2018 (Planalto)](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/_ato2015-2018/2018/lei/l13709.htm)
- DiffSinger (openvpi) disclaimer: "Any organization or individual is prohibited from using any functionalities included in this repository to generate someone's voice without his/her consent, including but not limited to government leaders, political figures, and celebrities" — [openvpi/DiffSinger](https://github.com/openvpi/DiffSinger)
- SOME disclaimer: prohibited to use "any recordings obtained without consent from the provider as training data" — [openvpi/SOME](https://github.com/openvpi/SOME)
- DiffSinger provides `drop_spk.py` to remove speaker embeddings from a checkpoint "for data security reasons when distributing models" — [DiffSinger Getting Started](https://github.com/openvpi/DiffSinger/blob/main/docs/GettingStarted.md)

#### EU AI Act (relevant if the app or its outputs are distributed in the EU)
- Art. 50(2): "Providers of AI systems, including general-purpose AI systems, generating synthetic audio, image, video or text content" shall ensure outputs "are marked in a machine-readable format and detectable as artificially generated or manipulated"; Art. 50(4): deployers of deep fakes "shall disclose that the content has been artificially generated or manipulated", limited for "evidently artistic, creative, satirical, fictional or analogous" works to disclosure "that does not hamper the display or enjoyment of the work"; applies from 2 August 2026 — [AI Act Article 50](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/50/)
- Commission draft guidance on Art. 50 was published 8 May 2026 (consultation to 3 June 2026); deepfake labels must be perceptible without technical aids and "a purely machine-readable marker is not sufficient on its own" for deployers — [SRD Rechtsanwälte](https://www.srd-rechtsanwaelte.de/en/blog/deepfake-labelling-under-the-ai-act-what-the-new-eu-guidance-clarifies)

#### Character and voice-product licences
- Crypton characters (Miku, Rin, Len, Luka, MEIKO, KAITO) "are licensed under both CC BY-NC and PCL"; the CC BY-NC grant "only applies to the original illustrations"; "Your use must be for noncommercial purposes only"; "You need separate permission from Crypton for commercial uses"; music is not covered and composers using vocal tracks must follow "each software's Terms and Conditions of its End User License Agreement"; no violent or sexual depictions — [piapro.net For Creators](https://piapro.net/license)

- Kasane Teto UTAU voice terms: free for non-commercial use by individuals or companies; individuals may post songs and sell their own songs (events, BOOTH); paid streaming and video ad revenue are "currently not treated as commercial use"; commercial use of the library needs prior separate permission (via the Piapro inquiry desk); no credit required and the voice need not be tied to the character; selling or distributing all or part of the voicebank, modified or not, without consent is prohibited; the Synthesizer V AI Teto Lite may not be monetized; AI training/voice conversion is not mentioned — [kasaneteto.jp voice terms](https://kasaneteto.jp/guidelines/voice.html)

### Inferences
- Voicebank creation should start with a written release from each singer (the user himself or hired singers) covering recording, AI model training, distribution of the voicebank/model, commercial use of outputs, credit, revocation and deletion — and recordings should be handled as personal (possibly biometric) data under the LGPD.
- Never train on Miku/Crypton outputs (explicitly forbidden) and do not train on Teto or other UTAU banks without written permission; using these characters' names or images in the app's branding would also need permission (PCL/CC BY-NC are non-commercial).
- For a product that may become commercial, avoid NC-licensed assets in the shipped pipeline: train or fine-tune a vocoder only from owned data (or verify the vocoder licence directly with OpenVPI), avoid M4Singer/GTSinger-trained weights, and avoid RAVE-family tools; prefer MIT/Apache/BSD components (OpenUtau, DiffSinger code, SOME, RMVPE, ONNX Runtime, iPlug2/DPF, VST3 SDK, CLAP).
- If outputs or the app reach EU users after 2 August 2026, embed a machine-readable "AI-generated" marker in rendered files (metadata and/or watermark) and offer an audible/visible disclosure option; music is likely to fall under the lighter "evidently artistic" regime for deployers, but the provider-side marking duty still applies to the tool.
